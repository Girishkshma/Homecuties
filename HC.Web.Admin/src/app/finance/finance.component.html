Finance

The shop's own books for a period: what the customers paid, what was given back, what the gateway kept for taking it, what the couriers were paid, and what the shop's own margin on the goods was.

Both days empty is the month to date - the stretch the dashboard's monthly tile reads. A period wider than a year is read a year at a time, and a period whose days run backwards is read as the single day it names. Everything is read at the day it belongs to: a payment on the day the gateway took it, a refund on the day it was sent back, a settlement line on the day it reached the bank - which is why the two sides of one week legitimately differ.

The gateway's charge and the GST on it are the figures Razorpay reports and deducts at settlement, and the GST on that charge is input credit the shop can set against the tax it owes - which the GST section below counts. Every line here is money that moved, and what the customers paid includes the GST the shop holds for the government, so it is not all the shop's money either. The goods line is the margin the shop itself declared on each product ('Profit margin %' on the product form, read as the profit's share of what the customer paid) - nothing here records what the goods cost to buy, so that line is a declaration while every line above it is a recorded one.

The sales above are what the customers actually paid, so they include this tax: set aside what the period owes and {{ leftAfterTax | currency:'INR' }} of the 'left after the goods' line is what the period was really worth to the shop. The tax is worked out from the rates the order lines recorded, at the rate the checkout charged (the CGST rate, which is the tax the customer really paid), and each refund gives back its own order's share of it. A negative 'owes' figure is not an error - a period carrying more input credit than tax offsets the difference against a later one.
